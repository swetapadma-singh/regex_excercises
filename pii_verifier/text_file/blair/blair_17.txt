complete verbal explanation of the examination results, diagnoses and treatment options. The risks, benefits and alternatives were

reviewed. The procedure(s) was discussed with the patient along with the risks, benefits and alternatives, including but not limited to
bleeding, infection, and neurologic injury, failure to relieve pain and worsening pain. The patient understood, all questions were
answered, and informed consent was obtained.

The patient was placed in the prone position on the injection table with a lumbar roll in place under the abdominal area.  The
lumbar area was prepped and draped in a sterile fashion.  Using a sterile technique, the superficial skin was anesthetized with 2%
Xylocaine.  An 18G 3.5” Tuohy needle was introduced into the  posterior epidural space under fluoroscopy using a midline

approach and loss of resistance technique at the ____L5-S1____ interlaminar space.  A dry tap was obtained.  An epidurogram
without dural puncture was obtained using omnipaque contrast confirming posterior epidural placement of the needle. Superior
epidural flow pattern was noted.  No intravascular or intrathecal flow pattern was noted. No blood or CSF was aspirated. AP and
lateral x-rays were taken for interpretation and documentation.

After this, a total of 40 mg Kenalog and 3 cc 0.5 % PF Xylocaine was injected into the epidural space without any resistance.

The needle was subsequently removed.  The patient tolerated the procedure well.  The patient’s vital signs were monitored during
this procedure and remained within normal limits.  There were no complications.  Post procedure vital signs remained normal as
well.

procedure instruction sheet was given. The patient was monitored for 30 minutes in a recovery area before discharged home in a
